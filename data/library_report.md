The personal digital library of understudied Latin and Greek texts contains 370 documents with a total of 312,467 segments, of which 325,026 have been translated (96.1%). The collection spans from the 3rd century to the 13th century, covering a wide range of eras including late-antique, medieval, and some classical and ancient texts. The majority of the documents are in Latin, with 357 out of 370, while there are also 13 Greek texts included.

The library primarily draws from several key sources, with Musa Medievalis being the most dominant, comprising 308 documents. This source focuses on medieval Latin poetry, including works by authors like Aegidius Columna Romanus. Other significant sources include ALIM (30 documents), which covers medieval Italian Latin, and DigilibLT (13 documents) for late-antique Latin texts. Patristic Greek texts are represented through the PG Corpus (7 documents), while Perseus and The Latin Library contribute a smaller number of documents each.

In terms of language distribution, Latin texts overwhelmingly make up the majority (357 documents), followed by Greek (13 documents). The era breakdown shows that the medieval period is the most represented, with 335 documents, closely followed by late-antique texts (32 documents).

Regarding genres, poetry is the most common type, with 308 documents. Unclassified texts account for 58 documents, while other genres such as scholastic works, inscriptions, histories, biographies, and others are present but in smaller quantities.

Among the largest works in the library, "De regimine principum" by Aegidius Columna Romanus stands out with 47,667 segments, followed closely by the "Reportatio lecturae super libros I-IV Sententiarum" with 24,490 segments. Other notable large works include various Patrologia Graeca volumes and medieval Latin texts such as "Liber de temporibus et etatibus" and "carmina" by Alcuinus. These large works significantly contribute to the richness and depth of the library's content.
